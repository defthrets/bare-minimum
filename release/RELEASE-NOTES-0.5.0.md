Hunger, thirst and sleep on the game's own clock, with health, armour and energy standing in the same row. 157 of the 201 things you can buy have a picture of their own.

## What is new in 0.5.0

**He drops the empty.** A can that vanishes out of a man's hand the instant he stops drinking was the one moment this mod stopped modelling anything. Now he drops it and it stays where it falls — a can for a drink, a bottle for a beer, and **whatever he ate it off** where he ate it off something: a bowl of chilli leaves a bowl, a slice of pie leaves the plate, a sundae leaves the glass. A slice with nothing under it in its own picture leaves the wrapper instead, which is judged per item, because only the picture knows.

It is capped at twelve and the oldest goes when the newest lands — props are a limited resource in this game, and a mod that adds one every time you have a drink with nothing taking them away eventually costs somebody their traffic. A reload does not sweep them up: they are handed back to the game, which clears them on its own terms like any other rubbish in the world.

**He actually smokes it.** The smoking dictionary holds three clips and the mod played the first one on a loop — which is the clip where he stands there holding it. All three are played now, one after another, for twenty seconds, and he can walk about while he does. The clip itself says when it has finished, so none of the three had to be timed by hand.

**A vending machine is not a shop.** He drinks it standing at the thing, the way the game always has, instead of putting it in his pocket. And the machine's own reach-and-press gets to finish first — it was being replaced on the very next line, so the press never played and whatever he bought appeared in his hand out of the air.

**Black out at the wheel and the police always come.** The kerbside wake-up is a gamble on purpose: you chose to park in the street and sleep, and something that happens every time stops being a hazard. Passing out is not that. You did not choose the spot, the car is stopped dead in a lane you were driving down, and that gets found. They ask what happened rather than telling you to move along.

**Two switches for the bars' movement**, one for each half of it: what lives inside the fill, and the fill's own surface. There were sliders for both and a slider at 0 is not off — one stopped the specks moving and still drew them, the other flattened the wobble and left the jolt. Turning the insides off also costs the frame fewer rectangles, which is a real lever if you run a lot of HUD mods.

**And the HUD's decoration comes back.** One frame over the shared rectangle line — a menu opening — priced the decoration at three times what it actually costs, and the release test could then never be met, so the cells, crumbs, bubbles and streaks went for the rest of the session. It is measured on an ordinary frame now, and a trim needs three frames in a row rather than one.

## Which zip

- **`BareMinimum-0.5.0.zip`** — the mod on its own. Take this one if you already run other SHVDN mods.
- **`BareMinimum-0.5.0-full.zip`** — the same, with ScriptHookVDotNet bundled alongside it. Take this one if this is your first script mod.

Neither contains **ScriptHookV**, which is Alexander Blade's and has to come from his site.

## Installing

Unzip and drop the contents into your GTA V folder — the `scripts` folder merges with the one already there.

**Unblock the ZIP before you extract it**, or ScriptHookVDotNet refuses to load the DLL and says nothing about it. If you already run script mods, take the plain zip — the -full one bundles its own ScriptHookVDotNet and two of those fight.

No asset replacement, no `.rpf` edits. Legacy and Enhanced from one build.

Everything is in `BareMinimum.ini` — 220 settings — and most of it is also on the in-game menu (**F11**), live. Your pocket is **F12**, and **E** is everything else.
